# Shot length and story shape
> How long a shot should be and how a short children's film is built from shots. Unmeasured: from the first runs.

**When to use:** planning the shot list.

**How:** 3 to 6 seconds is typical, 2 to 8 is allowed. Start the film with an establishing shot (wide: the
place, characters small, 3 to 5 s) and any scene in a new place too. Use closer, quieter shots for lines: one idea per shot, at most one
line (rarely two). End the film on a calm shot (a hold or a slow pull_out). A 15 second film is
3 to 5 shots; a 60 second film is 10 to 14. Shot seconds must add up to the target within 10 percent.

**What fails and why:** shots under 2 s cannot hold a motion and a line. A shot with three lines and
four motions is too much for ages 4 to 6. This note is a starting rule, not a measured one.

**Holds and timing:** a line needs its length (the recording, plus 0.6 s before and after: code fits
the shot to the recording). Give a silent reaction shot 2 to 3 s so the feeling lands. Hold the
last shot at least 3 s, calm, so the film ends rather than stops. Vary lengths: 3, 4, 3, 5, not
4, 4, 4, 4. A 15 s film: establishing 3 to 4 s, two or three talking/reaction shots, a calm 3 s end.
