# Emotion arc
> Every film and every scene has a shape: a quiet start, a rise to a peak, a calm end. Name each character's emotion in every shot.

**When to use:** before you list the shots: decide the arc, then give each shot an `emotion`.

**The shape (15 s film, 4 to 5 shots):**
1. *Opening, calm or curious.* The place, the characters at rest: nothing is wrong, something is
   about to catch their eye.
2. *Discovery, wonder or curiosity.* The thing appears; a small surprise.
3. *Peak, joy, excited or proud.* The action: they do the thing, it works. The biggest motion
   (`jump`, `clap`, `laugh`) belongs here and only here.
4. *Landing, calm, joy or proud.* A slow, warm hold: goodbye, a wave, a pull_out. Never end on a
   surprise, a worry or a jump.
Longer films repeat the rise inside each scene, a little higher each time.

**Rules the code checks:** at least 3 different emotions in a film of 4+ shots (2 for 3 shots); the
first shot is calm or curious; some later shot is a peak (joy, excited, wonder, proud); the last shot
is calm, joy or proud; sad, worried and grumpy together fill at most 30 percent of the seconds.

**A small worry is allowed** (a wobbly stone, a balloon stuck) when it resolves two shots later; the
child learns the world is safe by watching it be fixed.

**Two characters feel different things at the same time** and that is good: one worried, one
cheerful and encouraging (Pim worried, Mia cheering). Give the encourager a bright emotion and the
other the worry; both resolve to joy at the peak.

**What fails and why:** a film where everyone is `joy` in every shot has no arc and no peak, so it
is flat even when the faces are pretty. An ending on `surprised` leaves the child unsettled.
